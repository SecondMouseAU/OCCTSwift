What the eleven modes establish
-------------------------------

1. The distance is real and it is the kernel's own. Mode 0: LowerDistance() is exactly 5 on two
   planes built 5 apart, with IsParallel() true and NbExtrema() 1. Mode 2 repeats it over the
   planes' own +/-1e100 domain rather than a trimmed square, so the verdict belongs to the geometry
   and not to the UV box the caller passed.

2. The points really are absent, they are not merely withheld. Mode 1 asks the same object for
   NearestPoints() immediately after reading the distance and the process dies with SIGSEGV. So
   "distance yes, points no" is the kernel's own shape for this input, not a policy this package
   invented, and an API that offers the distance alone is reporting exactly what OCCT computed.

3. "Nothing reported" is a different outcome and stays distinguishable. Mode 4's two crossing
   planes intersect, so there is no extremum at all: NbExtrema() is 0 and LowerDistance() itself
   raises Standard_OutOfRange. (StdFail_NotDone_Raise_if at the top of LowerDistance is compiled
   out by No_Exception; what raises is the inline bounds check inside
   NCollection_Sequence::Value when myIndex is 0. Same mechanism as #2801.) A wrapper therefore
   has to test NbExtrema() before calling LowerDistance(), exactly as OCCTCurve3DDistanceToSurface
   already does, and the refusal it returns is distinct from any distance.

4. A zero is not automatically a fabrication. Mode 5's two coincident planes are parallel with a
   correct, computed LowerDistance() of 0. Mode 0 and mode 5 come out of the same code path with
   two different numbers, so a wrapper that returned a zero-initialised struct would be caught by
   mode 0's 5 and a wrapper that refused every zero would be wrong about mode 5.

5. The non-parallel path is untouched and still returns points. Mode 3, two spheres of radius 3 and
   5 with centres 20 apart, gives IsParallel() false, two extrema, 12.000000001961356 as the lower
   distance, and a NearestPoints() pair whose own separation is the same number to every digit.
   That is what makes modes 0 to 2 evidence about the parallel branch rather than about this build's
   Points() in general.

6. The asymmetry the issue names is confirmed at the layer the bridge calls. Mode 6, a line 5 above
   a plane through GeomAPI_ExtremaCurveSurface, reports IsParallel() true and LowerDistance() 5, and
   OCCTCurve3DDistanceToSurface already returns that number to Swift. Mode 7 is its non-parallel
   control at 37. Curve-surface has always answered; surface-surface is the one that refused.

7. IsParallel() is NOT "the two surfaces are everywhere equidistant", and modes 8 and 9 exist
   because that is the obvious assumption to make. Coaxial cylinders of radius 3 and 8, and
   concentric spheres of radius 3 and 8, are everywhere exactly 5 apart, and BOTH report
   IsParallel() false, NbExtrema() 2 and a real point pair. Read afterwards rather than before,
   which is the wrong order and is recorded as such: Extrema_ExtSS::Perform reaches myExtElSS only
   in its Plane x Plane arm (Extrema_ExtSS.cxx:120-127) and sends every other pair to
   Extrema_GenExtSS, which never sets myIsPar; and Extrema_ExtElSS assigns myIsPar = true in its
   gp_Pln/gp_Pln overload alone (Extrema_ExtElSS.cxx:49), every other overload assigning false.
   So in this kernel the surface-surface parallel case is exactly two parallel planes. This is the
   same narrowness #636 records for Extrema_ExtCC one class over, arrived at independently.

8. A LIMIT OF THE TEST SUITE, found by injection rather than by reading, and recorded because it
   will not be visible from the tests. Mode 10 exists because replacing LowerDistance() with
   Distance(1) in the bridge failed no test at all. LowerDistance() is
   sqrt(SquareDistance(myIndex)) where myIndex is the index of the smallest square distance, so the
   two differ only where the solver does not find the minimum first. Over fourteen fixtures,
   spheres near and far, coaxial and concentric pairs, sphere/plane, cylinder/plane, mixed types,
   swapped argument order and trimmed UV, index 1 is the minimum in twelve, and in the two where
   it is not the gap is 2.22e-16. So NO fixture found here separates the two at a testable
   magnitude, and the case for reading LowerDistance() rather than Distance(1) rests on the source
   (GeomAPI_ExtremaSurfaceSurface.cxx:225-230) and not on a red test. Anyone adding a fixture that
   does separate them should add the assertion with it.

   Two incidental measurements from the same sweep, neither chased here: a sphere of radius 3 and
   an untrimmed plane 9 above it reports ONE extremum at 12, which is the far side of the sphere
   rather than the near one at 6; and every near-touching pair reports its zero as 1e-11 to 1e-16
   rather than as 0, so an equality test against zero is the wrong shape for anything but the
   coincident-plane case in mode 5, which is exact.

Second construction
-------------------

#2840's probe measured Extrema_ExtSS and Extrema_ExtCS directly and reported SquareDistance(1) == 25
for the same two planes. This probe measures one layer up, through the GeomAPI classes the bridge
actually builds, and reports LowerDistance() == 5. sqrt(25) == 5, and the two constructions agree.
They also agree on the point read: 25 with no point pair there, 5 with a SIGSEGV here.
