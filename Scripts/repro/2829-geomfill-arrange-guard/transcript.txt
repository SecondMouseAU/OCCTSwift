#2829 ground truth: GeomFill_BSplineCurves / GeomFill_BezierCurves arrange and refuse four
boundary curves, and what the kernel we ship does instead of refusing.

Built and run 2026-09-29 against the PINNED asset, v4.0.0-kernel.2
(.build/artifacts/<pkg>/OCCT/OCCT.xcframework/macos-arm64), not the local Libraries/ build:

  clang++ -std=c++17 -ObjC++ -w -I"$L/Headers" -L"$L" -lOCCT-macos \
    -framework Foundation -framework AppKit -lz -lc++ probe.mm -o probe

Each section runs as its own process (probe <A..J>) because three of them crash.

WHAT IT SETTLES

1. Arrange() really does arrange. Section [B] passes the same four square edges in a scrambled
   slot order AND with three of the four reversed, and gets corner-for-corner the same surface
   as the canonical order in [A]. Section [F] confirms it over all 24 permutations x 16 direction
   flips: 384/384 accepted. So "connected end-to-end in order (bottom, right, top, left)", which
   is what Surface.bsplineFill(curves:) documented, is not a requirement OCCT imposes.

2. The refusal OCCT documents is COMPILED OUT of our kernel. Init() ends with
   Standard_ConstructionError_Raise_if(!IsOK, "... Courbes non jointives")
   (GeomFill_BSplineCurves.cxx:287, GeomFill_BezierCurves.cxx:209). Scripts/build-occt.sh
   configures -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release, OCCT's BUILD_RELEASE_DISABLE_EXCEPTIONS defaults to ON
   (CMakeLists.txt:194-196) and adds -DNo_Exception (adm/cmake/occt_defs_flags.cmake:226-228),
   and Standard_ConstructionError.hxx:24-30 then expands that macro to nothing. The `bool IsOK =`
   that feeds it sits behind the same #ifndef, so Arrange()'s false is discarded and Init() reads
   CC1->Degree() off the null handle it never filled in: sections [C], [D] and [E] are SIGSEGV
   (exit 139), not a catchable Standard_ConstructionError. A bridge catch (...) cannot absorb it.

3. Both classes, and both supports. [C] is the BSpline flavour on a planar boundary, [D] the
   Bezier flavour on the same one, [E] the BSpline flavour on a periodic support (a quarter patch
   on a cylinder r=5). All three crash.

4. The transcribed predicate agrees with the kernel. occtGeomFillCurvesJoin in
   OCCTBridge_Surface_Fill.mm is Arrange()'s acceptance half, endpoints only. [F] is 384/384
   agreement on accepted input and [G] pins the tolerance: a gap of 9.9e-8 joins, 2e-7 does not,
   which is Distance(...) < Precision::Confusion() == 1e-7.

5. Two refusals that ARE real throws, so they were already reaching the bridge's catch (...):
   Coons style with fewer than 4 poles per direction after knot alignment
   (GeomFill_BSplineCurves.cxx:300, seen in [E]) and SetSameDistribution's PrepareInsertKnots
   failure. Those stay as they are.

6. A side collapsed to a point is accepted, by both flavours ([H]), so the guard must not refuse
   it. The BSpline Arrange has a degenerate-curve pre-pass (GeomFill_BSplineCurves.cxx:77-91) and
   the Bezier one does not (GeomFill_BezierCurves.cxx:118); the predicate carries the same flag.

7. The TWO-curve Init is a separate contract and needs no guard ([I]). It runs no Arrange, and its
   .curved branch refuses two curves that share no endpoint with a real
   `throw Standard_OutOfRange("... Courbes non jointives")` (GeomFill_BSplineCurves.cxx:545), which
   No_Exception does not touch and the bridge already turns into nil. .stretch and .coons take the
   same two curves as opposite edges and build a surface. That asymmetry was undocumented and the
   Swift wrapper defaults to .coons, so it is now written down rather than guarded.

8. The two values the Swift doc snippets print are measured through the same calls the snippets use
   ([J]): a Geom_BezierSurface is always 0..1 in both directions, so point(atU: 0, v: 0) is its
   (u0, v0) corner, and the cylinder patch's own bounds are u=[0, 1.5708] v=[0, 1], so 0, 0 is its
   corner too.
